# Learning Course – Computer Science: Reading paths and cross-references

The course remains a **reading-based course with no compulsory assignments, programming exercises, software installations or account creation**. The following sequence is a recommendation; individual lessons can be consulted independently.

## 1. Course structure and lesson counts {#S1}

| Level | Scope | Nature | Status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Main course M1–M6 | 33 lessons | Foundational course in computer science, servers, databases, cloud computing, AI and robotics | 33/33 complete |
| Bachelor’s electives M8–M10 | 9 lessons | Optional additional subject areas | 9/9 complete |
| Master’s core M7 | 6 lessons | Independent development of scientific and methodological knowledge following the foundational course | 6/6 complete |
| Master’s electives | 8 proposed topics | Optional future specialist lessons; no texts yet | 0/8 planned |

The two additional elective lessons [M11-L1](../M11-L1.md) and [M11-L2](../M11-L2.md) bring the course to a total of 50 lessons.

“Bachelor’s” and “Master’s” refer here exclusively to **educational levels of depth**. This is not an accredited university degree programme or an academic qualification. Assessment reports and decision guides in the appendix provide additional reading and are **not** counted as further lessons.

## 2. Recommended continuous reading path {#S2}

1.  **M1-L1–M1-L7:** Concepts, computers, algorithms, software, data, networks and related subject areas.
2.  **M2-L1–M2-L5:** The progression from local software to servers, APIs, hosting and scaling.
3.  **M3-L1–M3-L6:** Data storage, replication, sharding, transactions and recovery.
4.  **M4-L1–M4-L5:** Workers, operations, security, costs and possible deployment models.
5.  **M5-L1–M6-L7:** AI foundations, learning, language models, agents, multimodal systems and robotics.
6.  **M8-L1–M10-L2:** Driven by individual interests and optional; no compulsory intermediate qualification before M7.
7.  **M7-L1–M7-L6:** Scientific reasoning, formal methods, distributed systems, security, AI evaluation and interdisciplinary synthesis.

Use the [course index](index.md) for the complete set of individual links. Chapters that are especially closely related are linked directly below.
